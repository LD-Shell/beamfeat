    The printed equation

    The screen certifies a set of formulas, and the equation is chosen from that set. By default a forward step adds the certified formula that improves the fit most, one at a time, and stops when the next would add less than parsimony_tol (0.001) to R². The options on this page change that choice. None of them changes the search or the screen, so the FDR guarantee on the certified set is the same under each. Choosing a setting compares the default with the two options most fits need, parsimony_alpha and input_test.

    A short equation (parsimony_alpha)

    On a noisy target the default keeps riders: the best of the many remaining formulas adds more than parsimony_tol to R² by fitting noise alone. parsimony_alpha=0.05 also stops the forward step at the first term whose gain fails to reject the null that chance reaches it. Here the target is x0 * x1 with 25% noise, among ten unrelated columns:

    import numpy as np
from beamfeat import BeamFeatRegressor

rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
X = rng.uniform(1, 6, (500, 12))
signal = X[:, 0] * X[:, 1]
y = signal + rng.normal(0, 0.25 * np.std(signal), 500)
print(BeamFeatRegressor(random_state=0).fit(X, y).equation())
print(BeamFeatRegressor(random_state=0, parsimony_alpha=0.05).fit(X, y).equation())

    y = 1.052*(x0 * x1) - 0.4088*((x0 * x1) / (x0 + x6)) - 0.1452*((x3 / x0) - x1) + 0.1295*((x5 / x0) - x1) + 0.04642   [4 of 50 certified terms; parsimony=None prints all 50; parsimony_alpha=0.05 prints 1, selection-row R^2 0.935 against 0.939]
y = 1.013*(x0 * x1) - 0.1608   [1 of 50 certified terms; parsimony=None prints all 50]

    The default's other three terms attach unrelated columns, x6, x3 and x5, to the product. At a signal-to-noise ratio of 1 the default printed 18 terms on average where one was true, and the check printed 1.1, with held-out R² slightly higher on average and a little lower in a few fits.

    On real data the check costs accuracy. Real targets tend to carry many modest effects, each adding little, so the step stops after a few terms (one to five on 10 of 12 real datasets), and held-out score fell on 9 of the 12 (diabetes 0.433 to 0.381, tips 0.472 to 0.433). Use it to look for a short law, not for prediction. It is a stopping rule, not a guarantee on the terms it keeps; the path in equations() is the same either way, and only the marked line moves. Choosing a setting and the guarantees page have the figures.

    Every column tested (input_test)

    The screen tests each term as a whole, so a term that attaches a junk column to the true one is still a true discovery: ((x0 * x1) / (x0 + x6)) is associated with the target through x0 * x1, however unrelated x6 is. input_test=True tests each column inside each term that reads more than one. The column's rows are permuted wherever it occurs in the expression, and its null is that it is independent of the target and of the term's other columns. A screened term is dropped before the forward step when the test fails to reject the null for any of its columns. On the same data:

    print(BeamFeatRegressor(random_state=0, input_test=True).fit(X, y).equation())

    y = 1.013*(x0 * x1) - 0.1608   [1 of 50 certified terms; parsimony=None prints all 8; input_test dropped 42]

    input_report_ lists every (term, column) pair with its p- and q-value, and null_rejected marks the pairs whose null the test rejected. A junk column correlated with a real one is not null in this sense, so its null can be rejected and the term kept; the guarantees page states what the test covers. It is opt-in because its time grows with the rows: here the fit took about four times as long, and n_jobs threads share the tests.

    The whole certified set (parsimony=None)

    parsimony=None skips the forward step and fits every certified formula. The guarantee then covers the printed equation itself (fdr_scope_ is "printed equation"), and the equation is long: 25 terms in the first example of Reading a fit, dozens on wide data. equation(max_terms=...) shortens what is displayed without changing the model.

    Re-testing the printed terms (parsimony_holdout)

    parsimony_holdout holds back that fraction of the selection rows. Screening and the forward step run on the rest, and the terms they return, fixed at that point, are tested again on the rows held back; terms whose null the re-test fails to reject are dropped. By default the re-test is the screen again (certify_test="marginal"), which tests each term's association with the target on its own. It rejects the null for a term that only rides on a true one, since such a term is associated with the target through the true one. certify_test="conditional" tests each term given the other printed terms instead:

    import numpy as np
from beamfeat import BeamFeatRegressor

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
X = rng.uniform(0.5, 3.0, (600, 8))
y = 2 * X[:, 0] + rng.normal(0, 0.5, 600)  # x0 alone

# Products only, and the whole screened set: every product of x0 with
# another column rides on x0, and the re-test sees them all.
options = dict(max_depth=1, binary_ops=("mul",), unary_ops=(), parsimony=None,
               parsimony_holdout=0.5, random_state=0)
marginal = BeamFeatRegressor(**options).fit(X, y)
conditional = BeamFeatRegressor(**options, certify_test="conditional").fit(X, y)
print(marginal.formulas())
print(conditional.equation())

    ['x0', '(x0 * x7)', '(x0 * x6)', '(x0 * x3)', '(x0 * x2)', '(x0 * x1)', '(x0 * x5)', '(x0 * x4)']
y = 2.009*x0 - 0.003251

    The conditional re-test is a permutation test for regression only, and its p-values are approximate; the guarantees page states its null and what its validity rests on. It works when the subset it receives is short. On a long subset whose terms nearly reproduce one another, no term adds anything given the rest, the re-test rejects no null, and the estimator returns the whole screened set with only the screen's marginal guarantee (certification_result_.n_selected is 0, and a NoDiscoveriesWarning says so). After the forward step that happened in about nine fits in ten at a signal-to-noise ratio of 1 (benchmarks/parsimony_holdout/). So after the forward step the conditional re-test needs parsimony_alpha (above), which brings the fallback under one fit in a hundred; without it the combination raises a ValueError. The example above re-tests the whole screened set (parsimony=None) and needs no parsimony_alpha. Both re-tests cost rows: hold back a fraction only when the selection rows number a few hundred or more.
