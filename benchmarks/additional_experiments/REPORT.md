# Additional experiments: results

Five studies extending the benchmark suite, plus three supporting
experiments. Every table is generated by `run_all.ipynb` from the JSON files in
`results/`. Hardware: 16 GB RAM, 22 logical cores. Row caps are seeded
subsamples, documented in `PROVENANCE.md` and overridable with `FULL_N=1`.

Parallelism: the tree baselines use library defaults (all cores); the
constructors are given the same through `N_JOBS`, except featuretools, whose
parallel mode holds a copy of the frame per worker. beamfeat has no
parallelism setting and runs single-threaded apart from BLAS, which its
recorded times should be read against.

## 1. Comparison on high-dimensional data

Nine datasets, p from 81 to 4,088, five splits each, 900 s per-fit budget.
No fit exceeded the budget. `beamfeat` is the estimator with its internal
ridge; `bf+ridge` is the transformer feeding the shared `RidgeCV` used by every
constructor.

Method means over all 45 fits: ridge 0.708, random
forest 0.697, LightGBM 0.671,
beamfeat 0.671, bf+ridge 0.671.
The estimator and the transformer agree to three decimals on six of the nine
datasets and to within 0.004 on the other three, as they should.

beamfeat beats both tree ensembles on five of nine datasets and wins outright
on tecator, where the signal is a ratio between absorbance channels that
threshold splits cannot construct. It trails ridge on ct_slices and
ujiindoorloc, where signal is spread across hundreds of individually weak
columns: ridge shrinks all of them, while a sparsity-oriented guarantee must
discard whatever fails a significance threshold. That is a property of
FDR-controlled selection, not a search failure.

`fdr_controlled_` was true on all 90 beamfeat fits. None of the 1,448 certified
formulas is deeper than three, and 1,434 of them (99%) are of depth two.

## 2. What the certified features carry

The comparison holds the consumer fixed so that differences reflect the
features. This grid instead varies the consumer, on the rows and splits the
comparison uses. The highest cell in a row is not a result: choosing a
consumer by test score is selection on the test set, and the reported method
keeps ridge, whose coefficients are the deliverable.

Trees cannot construct algebraic features but can consume them. On tecator a
random forest goes from 0.739 on raw channels to 0.992 on four certified
ratios. Where the tree baselines lead, most of the distance closes once the
consumer is nonlinear: ct_slices 0.677 to 0.858, ujiindoorloc 0.813 to 0.928,
superconduct 0.726 to 0.863. The features carry structure the linear model
cannot express. On the small-n datasets the tree consumers hurt instead
(eyedata, riboflavin, communities), which is why ridge remains the default.

This grid is deliberately asymmetric: no equivalent was run for the other
constructors, so it supports no claim that these features are uniquely useful
to trees. On tecator, featuretools reaches 0.987 with a shared ridge using
14,850 features against beamfeat's 3.6.

no errors. Recovery is scored up to algebraic value-equivalence (a selected
feature correlating above 0.999 with the planted column on a probe sample),
so equivalent rewritings count as recovered.

Depth is the minimal search depth required, which differs from the legacy
target names for three problems.

**Depth.** Under correlation, recovery is complete at depth 1, at or above
0.90 at depth 2 from beam 50 (d3_ratio_of_sum needs that width; d3_prod_of_sums
sits at 0.90 throughout), between 0.90 and 1.00 at depth 3 from beam 100
(adv_abcd_p8 needs that width), and degrades at depth 4: distance2d peaks at
0.20 and fails entirely under mutual information. Depth 3 is the practical
limit.

**Beam width.** Mean recovery under correlation rises 0.853,
0.913, 0.933,
0.933 at beams 20, 50, 100 and 200. The gain to 100 is
real — adv_abcd_p8 moves from 0.70 to 1.00 — and nothing is gained beyond it.
This is the invisible-intermediate effect directly measured: a weak
intermediate that ranks below the cutoff is lost permanently, and widening the
beam is what recovers it.

**Scorer.** Correlation is better in 21 of 60 cells, tied in 36, and worse in
3, all of them adversarial targets at narrow beams where the intermediates
carry no linear signal. It is also far cheaper: mean 2.85 s per
fit against 98.1 s, so the mutual-information arm consumed
16.3 of the grid's 16.8 hours.
Widening the beam is a better use of that budget than switching scorer.

**Failure mode.** Of 153 cells that did not recover the target, 148 certified
other features and 5 certified nothing. `fdr_controlled_` was true in every
one of the 1,195 cells with a non-empty selection; the five remaining cells
are empty selections, not violations. A missed target is a search loss, not a
false discovery: the features certified instead are genuinely associated with
the response, they are simply not the planted expression.

**Adversarial targets.** Three of the five targets whose intermediates have
zero population correlation with the response recover at 1.00 for every beam
and both scorers. The two that fail at narrow beams recover fully once the
beam reaches 100.

## 5. Selection procedures

Four procedures across four dependence regimes and three nominal levels, 100
trials each. FDR is reported against two null definitions: marginal
independence of the feature from the response, which is what the permutation
test addresses, and conditional independence given the other features.
The figures are from the run after the permutation-budget fix in 0.3.1, kept in
`results/selector_comparison*.json` and `.log`; the selector cell's printed output in
`run_all.ipynb` predates that fix.

**BH tracks its nominal level** (0.051, 0.095, 0.177 for 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 under
independence) with power at or near 1. **BY is four to six times
conservative** and costs almost nothing in power, which is what justifies it as
the default under arbitrary dependence.

**Knockoffs have no power at q = 0.05.** Knockoff+ cannot reject until at
least 1/q features carry positive statistics, so with ten true signals the
procedure is structurally unable to make a discovery at that level. Power
jumps to 1 at q = 0.10.

**The shared-factor regime is the important row.** Marginal FDR is zero for
BH and BY because under a shared latent factor every candidate is marginally
associated and the marginal null set is empty; conditional FDR is 0.90.
Knockoffs control the conditional error (0.069 and 0.170 at q = 0.10 and 0.20)
at the cost of an assumed covariance model. This is a scope statement about
the guarantee, not a defect in BH: the permutation null tests marginal
independence, and users needing conditional statements should prefer
knockoffs.

**The beam regime matches the pipeline** and there BH and BY reach power 1.00
with zero false discoveries, while knockoffs manage 0.00, 0.03 and 0.15,
because beam-generated candidates are heavily collinear by construction. They
cost about twice the p-value route here, and some forty times Benjamini–Hochberg's
cost in the two dependent regimes above.

A second run with a smaller candidate pool shows the detection threshold
directly: knockoff power at q = 0.10 under independence is 0.03 there against
1.00 above.

## 6. Split stability

Thirty independent search/selection splits per dataset. Selections are
compared up to value-equivalence; a class recurs when the same equivalence
class is selected across splits.

**Predictive performance is stable; the selected set is not.** Held-out R² has
a standard deviation below 0.01 across thirty splits on communities, tecator
and superconduct (the range across splits reaches 0.03), while Jaccard overlap
between selections runs from 0.000 to 0.188 on real data.
Recurring classes nonetheless exist and are interpretable: the planted
interaction recurs in 30 of 30 splits on three_way, the combination
(cement + slag) x log(age) in 27 of 30 on concrete, and one composite of four
element-property columns in 23 of 30 on superconduct.

Two datasets are unstable in performance as well: eyedata (sd 0.117, range
-0.073 to 0.462) and riboflavin (sd 0.157). Both have fewer than 130 rows,
and at that sample size the split itself dominates.

Tecator is the instructive case: no class recurs in more than 8 of 30 splits,
yet R² is 0.989 with sd 0.004. Adjacent absorbance channels correlate at
0.99988, so many band pairs are near-substitutes and the procedure selects
among equivalents. Low selection overlap under extreme collinearity is
near-equivalence, not instability.

## 7. Supporting experiments

**Label-permuted controls.** Five permuted replicates of riboflavin, the
highest-dimensional dataset, selected zero features each.

**Cost profile.** On superconduct, summary statistics account for 34% of a
fit, standardisation 15% and matrix assembly 10%; linear algebra is 1% and
permutation testing 0.5%. The cost is implementation-dominated, and the
dominant terms are vectorisable and parallelisable column-wise without
touching selection or testing.

**Search/selection split.** The search half carries no inferential role, so
its size is a free parameter. On superconduct, moving from an even split to
0.65 and 0.8 leaves R² within 0.02, at 0.726, 0.738 and 0.733. Fit times varied
too much between the two recorded runs (6.8 to 19.6 s per fit) to support a claim
about speed. At large n most of the search cost buys little,
and rows moved to the selection side buy testing power instead. The comparison
uses the default even split throughout.
