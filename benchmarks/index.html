    Benchmarks

    Every result here comes from a script in the repository and its archived run, or from the paper (TMLR 2026), which describes release 0.4.2. Results with a random component carry Monte Carlo error, so read them at the precision given.

    Most archived runs were made before 0.6.0 and 0.7.0 changed the search's defaults: constants inside expressions (fitted_ops), magnitude slots and the guard against quotients whose denominator comes near zero. Unless a result says otherwise, it was measured without them. Rerun at the current defaults, the formula-recovery suites recover the same formulas, the end-to-end calibration gives the same result, and Friedman #1 rises from 0.745 to 0.770. To reproduce an archived run exactly, install the release that made it (Installation). Where the paper's own numbers have moved, Reading the paper today says why.

    High-dimensional data

    benchmarks/additional_experiments/ compares beamfeat with ridge, random forest and LightGBM on the raw columns of nine datasets with 81 to 4,088 columns, over five random 75/25 splits each (the paper's Table 6). Mean held-out R², the best in each row in bold:

    dataset columns ridge random forest LightGBM beamfeat formulas
    tecator 100 0.957 0.717 0.756 0.991 3.6
    geomusic 117 0.805 0.739 0.739 0.805 9.6
    communities 100 0.650 0.638 0.632 0.641 11.6
    riboflavin 4,088 0.579 0.436 0.493 0.544 † 17.4
    eyedata 200 0.479 0.340 0.031 0.416 34.0
    superconduct 81 0.725 0.884 0.884 0.731 16.2
    blogfeedback 280 0.379 0.548 0.535 0.415 18.4
    ct_slices 384 0.852 0.981 0.981 0.687 † 14.0
    ujiindoorloc 520 0.949 0.995 0.993 0.812 † 20.0

    "formulas" is the mean number of terms in beamfeat's fitted equation.

    † Corrected since the paper: rerun with 0.5.0, beamfeat's mean R² is 0.340 on riboflavin, 0.664 on ct_slices and 0.816 on ujiindoorloc.

    beamfeat leads where the target is a compact algebraic function of a few columns, as on tecator, where ratios of absorbance channels are features that threshold splits cannot build. It trails where the signal is spread thinly over hundreds of columns, as on ct_slices, ujiindoorloc and blogfeedback: ridge keeps every column and shrinks it, while a screen with an error-rate guarantee has little power to reject the null for features that each carry a small fraction of the signal, and leaves them out. With the corrections, it is ahead of both tree ensembles on four of the nine datasets: tecator, geomusic, communities and eyedata. fdr_controlled_ was true on all 90 beamfeat fits of the archived run (the estimator and the transformer).

    On tecator, a random forest given the formulas in place of the raw channels goes from 0.739 to 0.992, on three of the five splits. On the small datasets, tree models given the formulas do worse than ridge, which is why ridge is the default.

    Tecator also shows what a single fit does not. In the paper's stability study, thirty fits with different seeds on one partition (the design of stability_report(..., resample="seeds")) held R² at 0.989, with a standard deviation of 0.004, while selecting 79 distinct features, counting value-equivalent ones once, none of them in more than 8 of the 30 fits. Adjacent channels correlate at 0.99988, so many band pairs are near-substitutes and each fit picks among them. One equation or one of many shows how to run the same check on other data.

    Tecator and the methods of chemometrics

    On spectra, chemometrics uses partial least squares (PLS) on preprocessed spectra, and kernel methods on derivative spectra are among the most accurate. benchmarks/tecator_chemometrics/, added after the paper, fits them on the same five splits, each tuned by cross-validation on its training rows:

    method held-out R² RMSE (% fat) size
    SVR, second-derivative spectra 0.998 0.66 all 100 channels
    kernel ridge, second-derivative spectra 0.997 0.79 all 100 channels
    Gaussian process, second-derivative spectra 0.996 0.85 all 100 channels
    beamfeat, second-derivative spectra 0.996 0.89 1 to 3 terms
    beamfeat 0.990 1.44 3 to 4 terms
    PLS, after the standard normal variate 0.971 2.33 5 to 18 components
    PLS, derivative spectra 0.958 2.89 12 to 20 components
    ridge 0.957 2.94 all 100 channels
    PLS 0.951 3.14 17 to 18 components

    beamfeat's error is about half that of PLS, the field's standard (46 to 62% of it). The kernel methods' error is about half of beamfeat's (46 to 59%), from all 100 channels and with no formula to read; on derivative spectra beamfeat comes close to them with one to three terms. Its formulas are mostly ratios of differences between nearby channels, and the channels it uses most lie at 920 to 946 nm, near the band around 930 nm usually attributed to fat.

    The data's documentation defines a split of its own: samples 1 to 172 to train, 173 to 215 to test, and two extrapolation sets, E1 (eight samples with more fat than any training sample) and E2 (more protein). beamfeat is averaged over five seeds:

    method test RMSE E1 RMSE E2 RMSE
    kernel ridge, SVR, Gaussian process (derivative) 0.54 to 0.55 2.69 to 3.76 0.41 to 0.44
    beamfeat, second-derivative spectra 0.64 1.74 0.43
    beamfeat 1.33 1.99 1.17
    PLS (raw, SNV, derivative) and ridge 1.98 to 2.20 8.08 to 8.47 1.66 to 2.19

    beamfeat had the lowest errors on E1, though eight samples make that a hint rather than a measurement. Releases before 0.7.0 missed E1 badly (RMSE 47.7) with a ratio whose denominator came near zero beyond the training samples; the search now rules such ratios out, and predict warns when a new row takes a feature far beyond its training range (New rows beyond the training data).

    Selection calibration

    • Selector level (benchmarks/selector_calibration.py). On Gaussian designs over 100 trials, Benjamini–Hochberg realised 0.046 ± 0.008, 0.084 ± 0.012 and 0.161 ± 0.016 at nominal 0.05/0.10/0.20, against its ceiling of q·m₀/m = 0.040/0.080/0.160; Benjamini–Yekutieli, stricter by a harmonic factor, realised 0.013 ± 0.005, 0.018 ± 0.005 and 0.046 ± 0.008. Power was 1.00 throughout. The bounds are computed from the design, and the script stops with an error if a bound falls below a realised rate's whole 95% interval.
    • End to end (benchmarks/calibration_study.py). Search plus holdout selection at nominal FDR 0.10, on 500 rows and 6 input columns of which 2 carry signal through their product, with noise at 5% of the signal's standard deviation: no false discovery in 200 replicates, a 95% upper bound of 0.015 on the true rate, power 1.000 and no fallbacks. Over 60 global-null replicates, nothing was selected.
    • Pure noise (benchmarks/selector_calibration.py). Over 100 global-null pipelines, where every selection is false by construction, BH returned features in 7 trials and BY in 1: false discovery proportions of 0.07 and 0.01, both under the nominal 0.10. On the same 100 datasets, BY's one selecting trial is among BH's seven, and an exact paired test rejects equal rates (p = 0.03). BY, valid under any dependence, is the default. The paper, on 0.4.2, reports 6 and 1.

    The guarantees page states what each procedure guarantees and under which assumptions.

    Formula recovery

    • Known formulas (benchmarks/run_benchmarks.py, one 70/30 split per dataset). Mean R² over the ten core-suite problems, and recovery of the generating columns over the ten synthetic problems that declare them; the two sets differ by one dataset each. beamfeat: mean R² 0.9989, fits of about 0.2 s, 10/10 recovered. autofeat: 0.9970, about 12 s, 8/10. LightGBM 0.9798, ridge 0.8442. On the purely linear problem, where feature construction has nothing to add, ridge ties beamfeat.
    • Feynman equations (benchmarks/feynman_panel.py, 12 physics laws, 0.1% noise). In the archived run, 10/12 solved at the SRBench criterion, and the exact symbolic form on 8 of the 9 laws that have one. The misses are limits of the search space: a depth-3 rational, a literal constant, depth-4 nesting and the Gaussian's exponential. With constants inside expressions (fitted_ops, the default since 0.6.0), the last two are found, the Gaussian as exp(-0.5*(x0)^2) and relativistic velocity addition as (x0 + x1)/((x0*x1) + 1): the exact form on 10 of the 11 laws that have one within the depth, still 10/12 solved, at 0.4 s per equation.
    • Heavy-tailed noise (Student t(2), infinite variance). The exact formula is recovered, at R² 0.956 against LightGBM's 0.866. The permutation test is distribution-free, so heavy tails do not affect its validity.

    Distractors and false features

    Signal hidden among ten distractor columns. A false feature is a returned formula touching only irrelevant ones; the count is over the five stress datasets that declare which columns their formula uses. Regenerate with python benchmarks/make_figures.py, which writes six other figures alongside it.

    • Stress suite (signal among ten distractor columns). beamfeat's false-feature rate, the share of returned formulas that touch only irrelevant columns, is 0.000 at noise levels from 5% to 50% of the signal scale and at n as small as 120, with recovery and the guarantee intact throughout. autofeat recovers the formula as often but returns distractor-only features on 2 of the 5 scoreable datasets. The comparison is a count rather than a mean, since a mean over five datasets carries a standard error of about 0.15, and autofeat's output varies between processes.
    • OpenFE on the same suite (its features feeding LightGBM, after shimming a removed scikit-learn parameter): a false-feature rate of 0.000 as well, but 0/5 formulas recovered and a lower R² on all five. It optimises a tree model's lift rather than equations.
    • Selection alone (knockpy on the raw columns, with the modified-FDR offset, the only satisfiable configuration at these dimensionalities): 0/15 formulas recovered, a core-suite R² at the ridge level (0.845), and distractor-only features on 2 of the 5 scoreable stress datasets. The difference from beamfeat is the construction step and the holdout.

    Real datasets

    Seven datasets, numeric columns only. beamfeat leads on mpg (0.864 against LightGBM's 0.853) and on tips, where gradient boosting overfits below ridge while the FDR screen keeps beamfeat at the top. It ties ridge on diamonds with one interpretable feature. It trails LightGBM on penguins, breast cancer and California housing (0.700 against 0.843, where the signal is spatial), and ridge on diabetes (0.376), where there is no nonlinear structure to find and beamfeat adds none.

    Known limits

    • Piecewise targets suit trees: on a threshold problem, LightGBM reaches 0.998 and beamfeat 0.808. When a tree model beats beamfeat by a wide margin, the signal is likely piecewise rather than algebraic. OpenFE also records 0.998 there, but bit-identically to raw LightGBM and from a feature touching only an irrelevant column, so the result belongs to its tree model.
    • Laws whose parts do not correlate with the target (benchmarks/search_stress/). With centred inputs, the parts of x0*x1*x2, and of x0*x1 among hundreds of columns, do not correlate with the target, so a search without magnitude slots can drop them before it combines them. Over 50 fits per cell, on inputs uniform on (-1, 1), x0*x1*x2 is recovered exactly in every fit with 5 columns but in 0.54, 0.18 and 0.10 of them with 20, 100 and 300, and x0*x1 in 0.32 with 1,000 columns; on shifted inputs the same laws are recovered in every fit. A wider beam does not close the gap. The magnitude slots, on by default for regression, recovered every lost cell but one crowded sum law (0.88), at 1.2 to 1.7 times the fit time, and left the real-data suites and the false-discovery rates essentially unchanged (guide).
    • Friedman #1 (benchmarks/friedman_decomposition.py). Over six draws, a representation oracle reaches 0.960 ± 0.003, a screening-admissible ceiling 0.874 ± 0.006, and the pipeline 0.780 ± 0.013. About 0.086 is lost to what marginal screening cannot see: the centred quadratic term is nearly uncorrelated with the target on its own, so a marginal screen has almost no power to reject its null. The other 0.094 is lost in the search. The guarantees page explains the marginal null.

    Independent comparison

    benchmarks/independent/ holds a 360-fit study (nine datasets, eight methods, five splits, average ranks and a Friedman test) with its harness, pinned environment, data, and a notebook that reproduces the analysis. Mean held-out R²: random forest 0.810, beamfeat 0.803, LightGBM 0.798, OpenFE 0.736, ridge 0.704, autofeat −1.561 and featuretools −2.478. The worst of each method's 45 fits: beamfeat 0.355, autofeat −103.2 and featuretools −57.3; no beamfeat fit fell below zero. beamfeat ran at about 60 times autofeat's speed, and fdr_controlled_ was true on 45 of 45 fits. The featuretools figures come from a harness that filled its non-finite test cells with 0; with the training medians, as the harness now fills them, they read 0.461 and −12.882 (see Reading the paper today).

    PROVENANCE.md records what reproduces and what does not. beamfeat's selections reproduce bit-identically, though the fitted model's cross-validated penalty can flip on marginal problems. autofeat sets no random_state and draws its decoy features from the global NumPy generator: six runs on one identical split ranged from +0.952 to −109.8.

    Relationship to autofeat

    beamfeat's design began from a code review of autofeat (Horn et al., 2019). It keeps autofeat's central idea, compact symbolic features feeding a linear model, and replaces exhaustive expansion with beam search, noise-threshold selection with FDR-controlled procedures, and upper-pinned dependencies with tested floors. The generate-then-filter design has a direct precedent in tsfresh (Christ et al., 2018), which filters mass-generated time-series features under Benjamini–Yekutieli control; beamfeat applies it to symbolic expressions on tabular columns. Migrating from autofeat maps one API onto the other.

    Three settings

    benchmarks/three_settings/ fits the default, parsimony_alpha=0.05, input_test=True and both together on the same data: a noise sweep on y = x0/x1 among unrelated columns, the release comparison's datasets and splits, and the paper's own studies with their own settings. Choosing a setting summarises it. In short, at noise as large as the signal the default printed a median of 9.5 terms, most of them reading an unrelated column, and parsimony_alpha printed the one true term in every fit; on the 12 real datasets the default had the best mean held-out score (0.693, against 0.675 with parsimony_alpha and 0.675 with input_test). At the paper's settings the comparison study's mean R² is 0.803 at the default, 0.784 with parsimony_alpha and 0.803 with input_test, against the paper's 0.803.

    Release comparison

    benchmarks/release_comparison/ runs the same benchmarks, data, splits and seeds through six releases, from 0.4.2, the paper's, to 0.8.0, on one software stack: about 30,000 fits. The paper's release against the current one, at their defaults:

    measure 0.4.2 (the paper's) 0.8.0
    Feynman panel, fits solved of 60 47 50
    search stress laws, exact recovery 0.883 1.000
    riders at signal to noise 1 (conditional FDR) 0.736 0.722
    7 real datasets, mean held-out score 0.725 0.727
    fit time, relative to 0.4.2 1 0.6
    peak fit memory, relative to 0.4.2 1 0.2

    The report has the releases in between, and measures each option against its release's default.

    Test suite

    1,079 tests at 97% statement coverage, with a 90% floor enforced in CI, including scikit-learn's full estimator-conformance checks.

    Reading the paper today

    The paper describes release 0.4.2, archived with its run records at 10.5281/zenodo.23417132. Some of its numbers differ from what current releases give, for three reasons: two bugs, figures from runs made under an earlier release, and speed. The archived records are kept as they were run. Comparisons added since the paper, such as the chemometrics baselines on tecator and the three settings, have folders of their own and change none of the paper's numbers.

    Two bugs, fixed in 0.5.0

    • Comparison harness. The harness filled non-finite test cells of the constructed features with 0, while the training cells got the training median. This understated featuretools, whose ratio features divide by columns that contain zeros. With the training medians on both, featuretools has a mean R² of about 0.461 rather than −2.478 in the independent comparison, a worst fit of −12.882 rather than −57.320, and one fit below zero rather than six. Every other method's row, and every conclusion about beamfeat, is unaffected (PROVENANCE.md).
    • Search. When max_candidates_per_depth bound at depth 1, the first round proposed parents in column order, so from about 258 inputs, pairs among the last columns were never proposed. The first round now proposes the inputs in order of their score, as documented. Three datasets of the high-dimensional study reach the ceiling. Rerun with 0.5.0, beamfeat's mean R² is 0.340 on riboflavin, 0.664 on ct_slices and 0.816 on ujiindoorloc, against 0.544, 0.687 and 0.812 in the paper; the archived riboflavin run was made with an earlier version, and 0.4.4 gives 0.523 there. With the ceiling raised so that every input is proposed, ct_slices and ujiindoorloc give the new results exactly; the old ones depended on which columns came first in the file. beamfeat now leads both tree ensembles on four of the nine datasets rather than five.

    Figures from runs made under an earlier release

    These are not bugs. The method is the one the paper describes, but the release that produced the figure is not the one it names, and the figures move little:

    • riboflavin, in the high-dimensional study, as noted under the search bug above.
    • The independent comparison. Its archived run predates 0.3.0, when the internal ridge penalty was fixed at alpha=1. Rerun with 0.4.2, beamfeat_ridge matches 37 of the 45 archived fits to four decimals, with a mean of 0.8034 against 0.8033 (PROVENANCE.md).
    • threshold_step, in run_benchmarks.py's robustness results, which were regenerated under 0.3.0. The paper quotes beamfeat's held-out R² there as 0.808, and 0.4.2 with the script's own settings gives 0.843. It reproduces the script's other 19 datasets and the Feynman panel exactly, and the five other robustness datasets to within 0.003 with the same number of terms (release comparison). That check cannot tell whether the release or the software environment causes the difference.

    Speed and memory

    Later releases are faster, so the paper's timings and memory figures describe 0.4.2. 0.5.0 runs the high-dimensional study 2 to 28 times faster per dataset without changing any feature, p-value or prediction, and a default fit on 1000 columns and 600 rows that peaked at 2.6 GB of memory peaks at 0.35 GB in 0.5.0. Over the eight timing items of the release comparison, 0.8.0 fits in about 0.6 of 0.4.2's time with a fifth of its memory. The changelog lists every change.
