    Changelog

    Notable changes to beamfeat. Format follows Keep a Changelog; versions follow Semantic Versioning.

    [0.8.0] - 2026-10-26

    Every fit at the defaults is 0.7.0's: on all 1,430 items of benchmarks/release_comparison/ the printed formulas and held-out scores are the same. What is new: screen_equation, three options that are off by default (residual_slots, max_search_rows, and the sign_shift operator with sign), and a note in the printed equation. An option is on by default only where measurement shows it does better than the default it would replace; the residual slots did not.

    Added

    • residual_slots: places added to the search's final ranking for the first terms of a greedy forward path through its candidates, so that a term much weaker than the leading one is not cut: on x0*x1/x2 + 0.5*log(x3) the default never printed log(x3), and residual_slots=10 printed the exact law in 33 of 60 fits (benchmarks/weak_terms_and_switches/). The path also compares each of its terms with the other transforms of its operand, which a depth may have dropped as near copies (log(x3) beside 1/x3). On its own it adds riders, a term built only from unrelated columns in 19 of 800 calibration-design fits against none, and it left held-out score within 0.005 on 8 of the 11 real regression datasets, raising it on one and lowering it on eyedata (by 0.89, through one split) and California housing (by 0.017). With parsimony_alpha=0.05 beside it the riders go and the weak term prints as often, which is the setting the docs give for a law with a weak term. Off by default; regression only.
    • max_search_rows: the search runs on that many of its rows, drawn at random, and its expressions are then checked on every search row; the screen still runs on every selection row, so its guarantee is unchanged. On 200,000 rows a search on 10,000 of the 100,000 search rows cut the median fit from 110 to 33 seconds. A product carrying 2% of the signal's variance, x4*x5, came out as x4 and x5 terms in every fit, as with the full search, and R² against the signal was 0.002 lower; peak memory fell by a sixth, since the screen and the final fit see every row (benchmarks/search_rows/). Off by default.
    • sign_shift, a fitted operator sign(x + a) (off by default; list it in fitted_ops), and sign, its form at zero (list it in unary_ops): a product with a switch, such as x2*sign(x0 - 0.7), becomes a candidate. On targets that switch at a threshold it printed the switch in 20 of 20 fits and predicted to R² 0.997 to 0.999, against 0.76 to 0.88 for the default and 0.983 to 0.996 for gradient-boosted trees; on the main suite's threshold_step it raised held-out score from 0.841 to 0.999. Elsewhere it changed little, though it lowered the classifier's accuracy on breast_cancer by 0.022.
    • equation() and latex() end with a note when parsimony_alpha=0.05 would print fewer terms, such as parsimony_alpha=0.05 prints 1, selection-row R^2 0.935 against 0.939, and equations() marks that line a. The count is the one a fit with that level gives: the check runs on the same path, from the same random stream, apart from the fit, which it leaves unchanged.
    • screen_equation: tests the equation another tool reports (PySR, Operon, gplearn, PySINDy and the like) term by term on rows the tool never saw, with the permutation test beamfeat's fits use under the Benjamini-Yekutieli or Benjamini-Hochberg step-up, and with input_test=True each variable inside each term of more than one. It returns an EquationScreen with the terms, their p-values and what was kept. It reads the equation with sympy, from the display extra.

    Removed

    • certify_test="conditional" after the forward step without parsimony_alpha, deprecated in 0.7.0, raises a ValueError. Pass parsimony_alpha=0.05, or parsimony=None to re-test the whole screened set. The studies in benchmarks/parsimony_holdout/ that ran the combination reproduce with 0.7.0.

    [0.7.0] - 2026-10-25

    Two changes to fits, under "Changed": magnitude_slots="auto", 10 beam slots for regression, and a guard in the search against quotients whose denominator comes near zero. With magnitude_slots=0 a fit gives 0.6.1's features, p-values and predictions on 1,243 of the release comparison's 1,430 items; the others are fits whose search met such a quotient. Predictions warn when a new row takes a feature far beyond its training range (under "Added"). The code is split into smaller modules, and the conditional re-test after the forward step without parsimony_alpha is deprecated.

    Added

    • ExtrapolationWarning: predict, transform and the probability methods warn when a new row takes a feature more than one width of its training range beyond that range, and say on how many rows, which features and how far; the predictions are unchanged. feature_ranges_ holds each feature's range on the training rows. A model saved with an earlier release has no ranges and predicts as before, without the check. On tecator's own split it was raised only where a fit missed the high-fat extrapolation set by far, and on no row of the test set.

    Changed

    • magnitude_slots="auto" is the default, where it was 0: 10 beam slots for regression and none for classification, whose labels carry no magnitude. The slots keep candidates whose magnitude tracks the target's, so that the search recovers laws whose parts do not correlate with the target, such as x0*x1*x2 with centred inputs. In benchmarks/release_comparison/, against 0.7.0 with the slots off, they recovered every such stress law (exact recovery 0.40 and 0.55 without them, 1.00 with), moved the held-out score of 10 of 12 real datasets by less than 0.005 (riboflavin fell by 0.011 and eyedata rose by 0.009), added a junk column to two more equations (27 of 40 against 25), and cost 1.19 times the fit time and 1.07 times the memory. The end-to-end calibration gives the result it gave without them.
    • The search rules out a quotient whose denominator comes near zero. A quotient or reciprocal whose denominator comes within the pole margin of zero on the search rows, or crosses it, is excluded as a value outside an operator's domain is. The margin is the one fitted poles keep since 0.6.0: twice the spread of the ten values nearest zero, and at least 1% of the range. Such a denominator can cross zero on a new row a little beyond the training data, where the quotient takes any size and either sign. On tecator's own split, one 0.6.1 fit predicted fat contents from -299% to 507% on the eight high-fat extrapolation samples, and 0.6.1's error there averaged 47.7 over five seeds, against 2.0 with the guard (benchmarks/tecator_chemometrics/). In benchmarks/release_comparison/ the guard changed the fit on 187 of 1,430 items, raised the held-out score on 3 of 12 real datasets (eyedata 0.110 to 0.312, riboflavin 0.506 to 0.527, tips 0.461 to 0.472) and lowered none by more than 0.005, left the riders on the calibration designs and exact recovery on the stress laws and the Feynman panel within two standard errors of 0.6.1's, and cost 5% of the fit time. In the pure-noise pipeline stress Benjamini-Hochberg now selects in 7 of 100 trials, against 6, and Benjamini-Yekutieli in 1. The guard is always on, so a fit that 0.6.1 made with such a quotient is reproduced only by 0.6.1.
    • The largest modules are split into smaller private ones, so that each stage of a fit can be read on its own. estimators.py keeps the three public classes; the fitting machinery they share is in _base.py, with _run_pipeline in named stages (rows, search, screen, choice of terms, final record) instead of one method of 378 lines; the forward step and its chance check are in _forward.py, the equation and LaTeX renderings and the equation list in _rendering.py, and the evaluation of new rows in _prediction.py. The built-in operators are defined in _operators.py, the place to add one. BeamSearch.run runs one method per depth instead of a loop of 264 lines. The public API and its import paths are unchanged. The split itself changed nothing that is fitted or printed: compared field by field on 327 fits across the benchmark datasets and options, its fits, predictions, p-values, equations, LaTeX, verbose output and warnings were those of 0.6.1, and models saved with 0.6.1 load and predict identically. Private helpers imported from beamfeat.estimators by name, such as _chance_p_value, now live in those modules. ARCHITECTURE.md maps the modules and a fit's stages.
    • ruff checks function size in src/: complexity 18 and 60 statements, the largest after the split, so that new code is split rather than grown.
    • The package's keywords, in pyproject.toml, CITATION.cff and .zenodo.json, name what it does (feature construction, multiple testing, Benjamini-Hochberg and Benjamini-Yekutieli, equation discovery, among others), and its classifiers add the operating system, the topics and Typing :: Typed. It stays in beta until 1.0.

    Benchmarks

    • release_comparison/ includes 0.7.0: its default, with the magnitude slots off, with parsimony_alpha, and with the conditional re-test alone and with parsimony_alpha, timed in a pass of its own beside 0.6.1.
    • three_settings/ fits the default, parsimony_alpha=0.05, input_test=True and both together on the same data: a noise sweep on y = x0/x1 among unrelated columns, the release comparison's datasets and splits, and the paper's own studies with their own settings.
    • tecator_chemometrics/, added after the paper, sets beamfeat beside the methods of chemometrics on tecator: partial least squares on raw, SNV-corrected and derivative spectra, and kernel ridge, SVR and a Gaussian process on derivative spectra, on the paper's random splits and on the split of the data's documentation with its extrapolation sets. beamfeat's error is 46 to 62% of that of PLS and about twice that of the kernel methods; on derivative spectra it is close to them, and on the high-fat extrapolation set its errors were the lowest.
    • selector_calibration.py prints the exact paired test behind the pure-noise comparison of Benjamini-Hochberg and Benjamini-Yekutieli: both fit the same 100 problems, and it tests the trials where only one selected.
    • tecator.csv now carries Tecator's permission note (additional_experiments/data/TECATOR_PERMISSION.txt), which its redistribution requires.

    Documentation

    • A "Choosing a setting" page sets the default, for prediction, beside parsimony_alpha=0.05, for a short equation, and input_test=True, for an equation whose every column was tested: what each prints, what it costs and what was measured. The Getting started tutorial ends on the same choice.
    • The user guide is split into four pages (Reading a fit, The printed equation, Search options, Data and prediction), with a quickstart before them and a table that finds the section for each parameter. The site's navigation groups the pages under Get started, User guide, Tutorials and Reference and API, and the changelog is on the site. Links in the text are underlined, and the mechanics behind an option fold away under it.
    • A "Citing beamfeat" page gives the paper, the software archive and, for each option, the statistical method to cite, with BibTeX checked against the publishers' records.
    • Each page of the site has its own title and description, a link preview (Open Graph and Twitter cards) and schema.org data: the software and its paper on the home page, an article on the others.
    • Every example in the quickstart and the guide was run again at 0.7.0, and the outputs that the magnitude slots and the guard changed are updated.

    Deprecated

    • certify_test="conditional" after the forward step without parsimony_alpha. On a noisy target the forward step hands the re-test riders it cannot certify, the re-test rejects no null, and the whole screened set is returned: in benchmarks/release_comparison/ 40 terms on diabetes and mpg, and 46 on the calibration designs at signal to noise 1, where the default prints 18 and the same re-test with parsimony_alpha=0.05 1.0 to 1.5. It warns with a FutureWarning and will raise from 0.8. With parsimony_alpha, or with parsimony=None, which re-tests the whole screened set, the conditional re-test is unchanged.

    Fixed

    • The parsimony_holdout re-test's warnings name the line of fit that raised them, as every other fit-time warning does, rather than a line inside the fit's internals.
    • A formula that divides by a fitted shift prints its divisor in brackets, (a / (1/(b - 2.5))), where it printed (a / 1/(b - 2.5)), which reads as (a/1)/(b - 2.5). Only the text changes; the expression, its values and to_sympy() were right.
    • to_sympy(), latex() and latex_formulas() without sympy raise an ImportError that says to install beamfeat[display], rather than a bare No module named 'sympy'.

    [0.6.1] - 2026-10-24

    Default fits are those of 0.6.0, feature for feature. This release fixes the memory of input_test, adds two options that are off by default (parsimony_alpha and KnockoffSelector(s_method="ci")), and adds a comparison of the releases since the paper's.

    Added

    • s_method on KnockoffSelector: "equicorrelated" (the default, as before) or "ci", the conditional-independence knockoffs of Liu & Rigollet (2019), which give each column its own s_j = c/(Σ⁻¹)_jj instead of one value set by the smallest eigenvalue. The guarantee is the same. In benchmarks/knockoff_s_method.py (fixed-X, nominal FDR 0.1, 50 trials) on 76 constructed columns, power rose from 0.000 to 0.217 with knockoff+ (realised FDR 0.000 and 0.038) and from 0.073 to 0.596 with offset=0 (0.096 and 0.112); on Gaussian block and factor designs the two were within a few points.
    • parsimony_alpha on BeamFeatTransformer, BeamFeatRegressor and BeamFeatClassifier, a chance check on the forward step (default None, which leaves every fit unchanged). On a noisy target the best of many remaining terms adds more than parsimony_tol to R² by fitting noise, so the step kept riders, 18 terms on average at signal to noise 1 in benchmarks/parsimony_holdout/. With a level, the step also stops at the first term whose gain fails to reject the null that it is no larger than the best gain the remaining terms reach against noise (200 wild-bootstrap draws of the residual, net of the terms already added; Fan & Zhou, 2016). In benchmarks/parsimony_holdout/parsimony_alpha.py (its designs at q = 0.1, 50 replicates per cell), parsimony_alpha=0.05 printed 1.1 terms instead of 18.4 at signal to noise 1 and 3.8 at 3; the conditional false rate fell from 0.70 to 0.05 and from 0.42 to 0.02, power to print the generating term went from 0.89 to 0.88 at 1 (1.00 at 3), and held-out R² rose by 0.007 on average at 1, though 9% of those fits lost more than 0.01. With parsimony_holdout and certify_test="conditional" it ends the re-test's fallback to the screened set (92% of fits at signal to noise 1, under 1% with the check). On real data it costs accuracy: in benchmarks/release_comparison/ (5 splits each) held-out score fell on 8 of 12 real datasets (tips 0.461 to 0.397, diabetes 0.430 to 0.381, riboflavin 0.506 to 0.306) and rose on one, eyedata, where the default's 32 terms overfit 84 training rows. Real targets carry many modest effects, each adding little, so the step stops after one to four terms. It suits a search for a short law, not prediction.

    Fixed

    • Memory of input_test. The expression evaluation behind it was a nested function that called itself, a reference cycle that kept the intermediates of every evaluation alive until Python's garbage collector happened to run. On California housing (7,224 selection rows, 133 term-column pairs) a fit passed 7 GB without finishing; it now finishes in 80 s using 0.55 GB, with the same terms. The undefined-row pass behind predict had the same cycle, holding its input until collected.

    Benchmarks

    • release_comparison/ runs the same benchmarks, data, splits and seeds through 0.4.2 (the paper's release), 0.5.0, 0.6.0 and 0.6.1 on one software stack, at their defaults and with the options of 0.6.0 and 0.6.1: about 16,000 fits, with speed and memory. 0.5.0 gives 0.4.2's fits on 1,415 of 1,430 items, 3.2 times faster with a fifth of the memory; 0.6.0 with fitted_ops=() gives 0.5.0's on all 1,430, and 0.6.1's defaults give 0.6.0's. archive_check.py refits 0.4.2 with the paper scripts' own settings: it reproduces the archived Feynman panel and 19 of the 25 run_benchmarks.py datasets exactly.

    Documentation

    • The Benchmarks page's "Corrections since the paper" is now "Reading the paper today", with the two bugs, the figures from runs made under an earlier release (riboflavin, the independent comparison and, newly noted, threshold_step: 0.808 in the paper, 0.843 from 0.4.2) and speed kept apart. The old link still leads there.
    • The Benchmarks page and the README link the release comparison.

    [0.6.0] - 2026-10-24

    0.5.1 was prepared but not released; its options are part of this release and listed under "Added". One default changes: the estimators fit constants inside expressions, fitted_ops (under "Changed"). The other new options are off by default (max_inputs=None, certify_test="marginal", magnitude_slots=0, input_test=False), and a fit that leaves them there and passes fitted_ops=() gives the features, p-values and predictions of 0.5.0, to the last bit, except with selector="knockoff", whose fixed-X statistic is fixed (under "Fixed").

    The re-test's figures below are from benchmarks/parsimony_holdout/: 48,000 fits of a global null and of y = a*b/c plus noise among 5 or 20 distractors, with and without a column correlated 0.9 with a, at signal-to-noise ratios 1 and 3, 300 and 1,000 rows, 200 replicates per cell, q = 0.1 and 0.2. A printed term counts as false in the conditional sense when it uses any column outside the formula, as a rider such as (a*b/c) * x7 does.

    Added

    • fitted_ops on BeamSearch, BeamFeatTransformer, BeamFeatRegressor and BeamFeatClassifier (on by default in the three estimators, under "Changed"; BeamSearch defaults to (), which leaves every result unchanged): operators with one constant fitted inside the expression, exp_scaled (exp(a*x)), pow (x^a), inv_shift (1/(x + a)) and log_shift (log(x + a)). At each depth each is fitted to each member of the previous depth's beam and to that depth's beam_width best candidates the redundancy check kept out of the beam (a linear near-duplicate need not stay one under 1/(x + a) or exp(a*x)), and as a factor of a beam member where the depth limit allows, by maximising |corr| with the target on the search rows. A fitted pole keeps a margin from the data, twice the spread of the operand's ten most extreme values and at least 1% of its range: the shifts' grids start at it, and pow fits a negative power, whose pole is at 0, only where the operand stays more than the margin above 0. The constant snaps to a simple value (0, ±1, ±2, ±3, ±1/2, ±3/2, ±1/3, ±1/4) when a likelihood-ratio test at level 0.001 fails to reject the null that it takes that value, never past the margin, and is exact in to_sympy() and latex(). At the end of the search, a kept feature with fitted constants gives way to a near copy with fewer (correlation at redundancy_threshold, and no near copy of another kept feature) when the same test, on the same rows, fails to reject the null that the copy fits the target as well; the simplest such copy is taken. Without that step, a constant fitted on the search rows bends an expression into a near copy of a simple law that outscores the law there, and the equation prints (x0)^(-0.02) for log(x0): at 10% noise, log(x0), sqrt(x0), 1/x0 and x0^2 were printed as themselves in 0, 0, 2 and 2 of 10 fits, and with it in 10, 7, 10 and 8 (10, 9, 10 and 8 with fitted_ops=()). The screen sees fixed expressions, so its guarantee is unchanged. Node.const, Node.n_constants, transform(..., const=...), OperatorSpec.constant_grid, pole_margins, SearchTrace.n_fitted and beamfeat.parametric support it.

    • max_inputs on the same four classes (default None): the most distinct input columns one feature may use, checked before evaluation. An expression of depth d uses at most 2^d columns, so at the default depth a value of 4 or more changes nothing. Measured at max_inputs=3 against 0.5.0, mean held-out R² over 5 splits:

    • gains on wide data with diffuse signal: eyedata 0.416 to 0.488, ct_slices 0.664 to 0.684, riboflavin 0.340 to 0.378;
    • losses: the twelve-law panel loses the gravitational law, which needs four inputs, and diabetes falls from 0.382 to 0.360;

    • one blogfeedback split falls from 0.533 to −2.14, where a three-input product extrapolates far past its training range on one test row.

    • SearchTrace.n_rejected_constraints, the candidates max_inputs ruled out at each depth (0 when it is off).

    • .zenodo.json, so that the Zenodo record of each release links the TMLR paper as the work the software supplements. Zenodo read the title, author and keywords from CITATION.cff but not its preferred-citation, so the records up to 0.5.0 do not mention the paper.

    • certify_test on BeamFeatTransformer, BeamFeatRegressor and BeamFeatClassifier: the test the parsimony_holdout re-test applies. "marginal" re-runs the screen, as before, and so rejects the null of a term that only rides on a true one, x0*x7 when the target depends on x0 alone. "conditional" tests each re-tested term given the others with a Freedman-Lane permutation test: the target and the term are residualised on the other terms and an intercept, the target's residuals are permuted, the statistic is the partial correlation, and the screen's step-up correction is applied at the screen's level. Its null is a zero coefficient in a linear model of the target on the re-tested terms, and its p-values are approximate: exact for a single term, otherwise resting on exchangeable errors of a linear model of the target on the other terms. Regression only; classification raises. At a signal-to-noise ratio of 3 it brought the conditional rate of the printed equation to 0.18 to 0.22, against 0.42 to 0.44 at the defaults and 0.53 to 0.62 with the marginal re-test, and in the fits where it rejected at least one null it printed 1.1 to 1.2 terms at a rate of 0.026 to 0.040 (q = 0.1), with power 0.98 to 0.99. When the re-tested subset is long and its terms nearly reproduce one another the test rejects no null, and the screened set, which carries the marginal guarantee only, is returned: in 87 to 92% of fits at a ratio of 1, where the forward step retains 18 terms on average, and in 18 to 26% at 3.

    • beamfeat.stability: stability_report(estimator, X, y), also exported from beamfeat, makes the paper's stability study (Appendix E) a package feature; by default n_splits=30, test_size=0.25, random_state=0 and n_jobs=1. It refits clones of a regressor, classifier or transformer on random splits. Each split's test part is kept out of every step of its fit and scored once after it, and the clones' seeds are spawned from random_state by SeedSequence, so the report is the same for every n_jobs. The StabilityReport holds:

    • each split's rows, formulas, equation and held-out score;
    • the distinct features and equations, with how often each recurs;
    • a summary that states the ambiguity plainly. On diabetes it reads "30 distinct equations in 30 splits; none recurs", with held-out R² from 0.389 to 0.575.

    Two features are one when their values on the rows of X correlate above 0.999 in absolute value, as in the paper, and an equation is the set of its features. The regressor and classifier are scored by their own score. A transformer is scored by a ridge regression (R²) on its features, or by a logistic regression (accuracy) for classification. The paper held one test part fixed and varied only the seed, so its spread is narrower: on diabetes, an R² standard deviation of 0.015 against 0.050 here.

    • resample on stability_report, which picks what varies. "splits", the default and the behaviour above, measures split-to-split variability. "seeds" keeps the first split's partition for every split and varies only the clones' seeds, as the paper's study did, and measures seed-to-seed variability. With the paper's transformer on diabetes it gives an R² standard deviation of 0.008 to 0.019 over five partitions, with 75 to 84 classes, the commonest in 8 to 18 of 30 splits. The paper reports 0.015, 79 and 22 for its own partition. On that partition, its own code with other lists of 30 seeds gives 79 to 88 classes, the commonest in 18 to 23.

    • magnitude_slots on BeamSearch, BeamFeatTransformer, BeamFeatRegressor and BeamFeatClassifier (default 0, which leaves every result unchanged; regression only, and a classifier raises): beam slots for laws whose parts do not correlate with the target, which the score ranks among noise, such as x0 and x1 in x0*x1 or the pairwise products in x0*x1*x2 when the inputs are centred. At every depth but the last, after the beam_width candidates the score keeps, up to that many more are kept, ranked by the absolute correlation of log(1 + |c|/rms(c)) with the same transform of the centred target or of its residuals; one residual is on the best-scoring columns kept so far, at most a quarter as many as the rows, since the residual on every input is empty once they outnumber the rows. When max_candidates_per_depth can bind at the first depth, the inputs are proposed in an order that interleaves that ranking with the score's. Measured at 10 slots on benchmarks/search_stress/, the three-way product on centred inputs went from 0.54, 0.18 and 0.10 to 1.00 with 20, 100 and 300 columns, the centred pair with 1,000 columns from 0.32 to 1.00, and x0*x1 + x2 with 300 columns and 250 search rows from 0.00 to 0.88; no cell got worse. The score is computed on at most 1,000 search rows, evenly spaced.     Fixed

    • The fixed-X knockoff statistic.     [0.3.1] - 2026-08-22

    Correctness release for the automatic permutation budget, plus two warning fixes. Selections are unchanged everywhere the previous budget was already adequate: the two widest problems in the high-dimensional study (riboflavin and superconductivity) return identical formulas, feature counts, flags and held-out scores, with fit times differing by less than the run-to-run spread. The budget binds in the selector calibration studies, where a small pool at a strict level is where the harmonic factor matters: rerun with the fix, the Benjamini-Yekutieli figures move in the third decimal (realised FDR at q = 0.05 from 0.008 to 0.013; power at that level from 0.988 to 0.989), and the figures in the README, the docs and benchmarks/additional_experiments/REPORT.md come from runs with the fix. Thirty-seven tests take the suite to 411 at 95% statement coverage.

    Added

    • verbose is now a level rather than a flag, and reports to stdout instead of through the module logger -- progress a caller explicitly asked for should not require them to attach a handler first, which is the scikit-learn convention. 0 (default) is silent and unchanged; 1 prints one line per stage, covering the split, the search, the screening, the parsimony step and the fitted equation, with the FDR flag on the result line; 2 adds per-depth search detail -- proposed, evaluated, kept, what was rejected and why -- and the strongest certified candidates with their p- and q-values. Warnings and diagnostics continue to use the logger, because those are events the caller did not ask for. Reporting now covers selection as well as search; previously any positive verbose logged search progress only, and the screening step, which is where the guarantee is actually applied, was invisible.

    Added (continued)

    • parsimony_holdout divides the selection rows a second time, so that the printed equation carries a guarantee of its own rather than being a subset of a certified set. Screening and parsimony run on the first part; the resulting subset is fixed at that point, so re-testing it on the part held back is an ordinary fixed-candidate screen and its guarantee covers the subset itself. Terms that fail are dropped; if none survives, an intercept-only model is returned with a visible warning rather than an uncertified equation. Off by default, because what it costs is rows: on 240- and 5000-row problems it returns the same terms at the same accuracy, and on a 71-row problem it loses twelve of nineteen terms. The estimator warns rather than proceeding when the selection rows cannot be split so that both parts hold at least ten. When a compact certified equation cannot be produced -- because the rows will not split, or because nothing survives the re-test -- the whole screened set is returned rather than a pruned subset of it. The caller asked for a guarantee over what is printed; the screened set supplies one and a pruned subset does not, so that is the honest way to degrade. Note that parsimony=None reaches the same corner for free, and on short samples is the better choice: a long certified equation beats a compact one whose terms were dropped for want of rows. The two-stage procedure has not been FDR-calibrated and is offered as an option rather than a measured result.
    • fdp_inflation_ reports |S|/|S'|, the factor by which pruning the screened set down to the printed equation can inflate the realised false discovery proportion. It was derivable from selection_report_ before and is now surfaced directly, since it is the number to quote alongside target_fdr.

    Added (continued)

    • Constant input columns are reported at fit time. Such a column standardises to zeros, so its association with the target is zero at every depth it can appear in and it can never be selected; that is the correct outcome and costs neither accuracy nor multiplicity, since the beam filters it before screening. What it does cost is legibility: in the output a stuck sensor or a column emptied by a bad join looks exactly like a variable that does not matter. The test consults no response values, so reporting it cannot bias what follows. It fires on real data -- 55 of 521 columns in ujiindoorloc, five of 385 in ct_slices, four of 281 in blogfeedback.
    • is_constant is exported. Constancy is judged relative to the column's magnitude rather than against an absolute floor on the spread, because an absolute floor makes the verdict depend on the column's units: a column varying over the whole range 0 to 1e-9 has a smaller variance than a stuck sensor reading 9.81, and any floor that keeps the first also keeps the second. The estimator and the diagnostic now share the one predicate, so the warning cannot come to describe behaviour the fit no longer has.

    Documentation

    • New tutorial, notebooks/04_reading_a_fit.ipynb. The first three notebooks plant an answer and confirm it is recovered; this one is about the fit you did not plant. On a pump-power problem hidden among noise columns it walks through the cases an applied user actually hits: why a deeper search returns a worse equation, why fdr_controlled_ stays True when noise columns appear inside a formula (the null is marginal, so a formula combining the true core with noise has a false null and selecting it is not an error), why depth counts composition levels rather than variables, how units remove the hitchhikers where statistics cannot, why unit coverage is all or nothing, where a constant factor goes, how to read selection_report_ against equation(), and how to tell an exhausted permutation budget from an absent signal. Runs in about 14 s.
    • Notebook 03 notes that knockoffs cannot prune a term out of an expression: selection operates on whole candidate columns, so switching the selector does not reach inside a composite. The conditional null invites that inference and it does not hold.
    • Notebook 01 builds its design as a DataFrame, so formulas read (a * b) / c rather than (x0 * x1) / x2, and demonstrates the verbosity levels.

    Fixed

    • The permutation count chosen when auto_permutations is on was sized to the Benjamini-Hochberg resolution bound 2m/q, while "by" is the estimator's default correction. Benjamini-Yekutieli requires the leading p-value to clear q/(m c(m)), so its bound is larger by the harmonic factor -- above five once m reaches the low hundreds. Below it the correction cannot reject at all, and the failure was silent: an empty selection reads as "no signal" rather than "budget too small". It bites hardest when few candidates reach the p-value floor together, since several tied signals relax the threshold by their count while a lone one carries it alone, which is why a sparse true set in a large pool was the exposed case. _required_permutations now takes its bound from the configured correction, and max_permutations rises from 100,000 to 1,000,000 so the larger requirement is reachable. The warning raised when it is not names the correction and offers correction="bh" as the cheaper alternative.
    • Fitting on a DataFrame no longer emits scikit-learn's "X does not have valid feature names" warning. The post-fit degeneracy diagnostic scores the estimator's own validated array, which carries no column names by construction; the warning fired on every named fit and pointed the caller at a mismatch of our own making.
    • units covering some columns but not all now warns. An unlabelled column is dimensionally unconstrained and combines freely with the labelled ones, so partial coverage leaves the check not binding on exactly the columns the caller did not vouch for -- while a mapping matching no column already raised. Labelling the known columns and leaving the rest blank looks like the careful thing to do, which is what made the silence worth removing. Pass "dimensionless" for genuinely unitless columns. The warning is raised once at fit, not on every transform or predict.

    Added

    • style parameter on equation() for both supervised estimators: "significant" (default), "fixed", and "scientific". "fixed" falls back to significant figures for any value that would otherwise display as zero.
